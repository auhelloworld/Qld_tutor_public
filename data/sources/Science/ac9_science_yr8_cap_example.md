## Level description

In Year 8 students are introduced to cells as microscopic structures that explain macroscopic features of living systems. They connect form and function at an organ level and explore the organisation of a body system in terms of flows of matter between interdependent organs. They continue to develop a view of Earth as a dynamic system, in which change occurs across a range of timescales. They classify different types of energy and describe the role of energy in causing change in systems, including the role of energy and forces in the geosphere. They learn to classify matter at the atomic level and distinguish between chemical and physical change. They understand that chemical reactions also involve energy. Students use experimentation to isolate relationships between components in systems and explain these relationships through increasingly complex representations. They consider the magnitude of properties and events and use appropriate units to describe proportional relationships.

### Inquiry questions

Inquiry questions can help excite students' curiosity and challenge their thinking. Examples include:

- Can we predict changes to the shape and position of continents?
- Are facts enough? How much does science communication matter?
- How can we tell if a substance has changed?
- How can we best measure what we cannot directly see?
- How is a leaf like a lung?

## Unit overview

| Unit | Title | Science focus | Duration | Overview |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| U1 | Our future health | Biological sciences | 10 weeks | Students investigate how understanding organs at a cellular level can inform research and development of augmented or artificial organs. They identify relationships between cell organelle structure and function, then transfer this understanding to tissues, organs and systems in plants and animals. Students create a physical 2D or 3D model showing how disease or injury disrupts the function of an organ or system. The unit supports critical and creative thinking, ethical reflection, and group-work capabilities. |
| U2 | Energy alternatives | Physical sciences | 10 weeks | Students explore whether claims about alternative electricity generation technologies, such as being the most efficient or green energy, should be examined. Solar, wind, hydro and geothermal energy are considered as potential Australian alternatives. Students build on Year 6 learning about energy and electrical circuits by exploring energy transfer and transformation in power generation, including sustainable design practices. They use science inquiry skills to evaluate claims about the capacity of technologies to meet society's energy demands. |
| U3 | Useful rocks | Earth and space sciences | 10 weeks | Students research evidence supporting the theory of plate tectonics and the role of plate tectonics in the rock cycle. They demonstrate how scientific knowledge changes over time as new evidence emerges. Students compare geological processes in Australia with more active geological regions, link rock formation processes to rock properties and uses, and research traditional geological knowledge and uses of rocks by First Nations Australians. The unit includes ethical access to and use of secondary knowledge and data. |
| U4 | Does it matter? | Chemical sciences | 10 weeks | Students use the language of chemistry to understand and describe molecular-level processes. They consider how predicting and representing chemical reactions supports innovation, such as discovering medicines, nanomaterials, new energy sources and ways to remove environmental pollutants. Students build on prior learning about pure substances and mixtures, use models, symbols and formulas to represent matter, compare chemical and physical changes, and identify energy changes in chemical reactions. |

## Assessment plan

| Assessment | Unit | Term/week | Technique | Mode | Conditions | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assessment 1 | U1 - Our future health | Week 9 | Investigation | Multimodal | Individual response; 1-2 minutes | Students create a physical 2D or 3D model to demonstrate their understanding of how the function of an organ or system may be impacted by injury or disease. They present their findings on the role of specialised cells, organs and body systems. |
| Assessment 2 | U2 - Energy alternatives | Week 9 | Examination | Written | 70 minutes, plus 10 minutes planning; supervised | Students analyse second-hand data on alternative energy sources and evaluate claims against the provided data. |
| Assessment 3 | U3 - Useful rocks | Week 9 | Investigation | Written | 500-600 words | Students research the evidence base that informs current understanding of plate tectonics and rock formation and use this to annotate historical explanations of the developing tectonic plate theory. |
| Assessment 4 | U4 - Does it matter? | Week 9 | Experimental investigation | Practical and written | Group work; individual response; 500-600 words | Students plan and conduct an experiment to investigate physical and chemical changes and show how they can be represented. |

## Achievement standard

By the end of Year 8 students explain the role of specialised cell structures and organelles in cellular function and analyse the relationship between structure and function at organ and body system levels. They apply an understanding of the theory of plate tectonics to explain patterns of change in the geosphere. They explain how the properties of rocks relate to their formation and influence their use. They compare different forms of energy and represent transfer and transformation of energy in simple systems. They classify and represent different types of matter and distinguish between physical and chemical change. Students analyse how different factors influence the development of, and lead to changes in, scientific knowledge. They analyse the key considerations that inform scientific responses and how these responses impact society. They analyse the importance of science communication in shaping viewpoints, policies and regulations.

Students plan and conduct safe, reproducible investigations to test relationships and explore models. They describe potential ethical issues and intercultural considerations needed for specific field locations or use of secondary data. They select and use equipment to generate and record data with precision. They select and construct appropriate representations to organise and process data and information. They analyse data and information to describe patterns, trends and relationships and identify anomalies. They identify assumptions and sources of error in methods and analyse conclusions and claims with reference to conflicting evidence and unanswered questions. They construct evidence-based arguments to support conclusions and evaluate claims. They select and use language and text features appropriately for their purpose when communicating their ideas, findings and arguments to specific audiences.
